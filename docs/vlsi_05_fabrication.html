Phase 5 — Semiconductor Fabrication From sand to silicon wafer to finished CMOS transistor. Heavily tested on NPTEL. Duration: 4–5 weeks.

5.1 — Crystal Growth & Wafer Preparation

  • Czochralski method: pulling a single silicon crystal from a molten melt — the industry standard
  • Float-zone method: higher purity, but only for smaller wafers
  • Modern wafer diameter: 300mm (12"); orientation typically (100) — lower interface state density than (110)/(111), which is why CMOS prefers it
  • Crystal defects to know: point defects (vacancies, interstitials), line defects (dislocations), area defects (stacking faults)
Fig 5.1 — A finished 300mm silicon wafer with an orientation notch. This single wafer, after hundreds of fabrication steps, will hold hundreds of individual chip dies.

5.2 — Thermal Oxidation NPTEL Core

TypeReactionSpeed/QualityUsed for
Dry oxidationSi + O₂ → SiO₂Slower, higher qualityGate oxide
Wet oxidationSi + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 2H₂Faster, lower qualityField oxide

46% of the grown oxide extends into the original silicon surface; 54% grows above it.

Deal-Grove Model: Linear regime (thin oxide): x_ox = (B/A) × t Parabolic regime (thick oxide): x_ox² = B × t B/A = linear rate constant (surface-reaction limited, dominates at short times) B = parabolic rate constant (diffusion limited, dominates at long times)

Growth rate depends on

  • Temperature — higher T grows faster (Arrhenius/exponential)
  • Pressure — higher P grows faster (high-pressure oxidation, HIOX)
  • Orientation — (111) fastest, (110) medium, (100) slowest
  • Dopant type — n+ substrate grows faster than p+ under wet oxidation
  • Chlorine — HCl addition improves oxide quality, reduces interface states

LOCOS vs. STI (isolation)

LOCOS (Local Oxidation of Silicon): nitride mask, suffers from the "bird's beak" encroachment problem. STI (Shallow Trench Isolation): etch a trench, fill with oxide, planarize with CMP — no bird's beak, dominant in every sub-0.35μm process, including everything you'll design today.

5.4 — Doping: Diffusion & Ion Implantation NPTEL Core

Fick's first law: J = −D·(∂C/∂x) Fick's second law: ∂C/∂t = D·(∂²C/∂x²) Gaussian profile (limited source): C(x,t) = (Q/√(πDt))·exp(−x²/4Dt) Erfc profile (constant source): C(x,t) = Cs·erfc(x / 2√(Dt))

Junction depth xj is where C(xj) equals the background doping. The Dt product controls the profile shape — this is a standard NPTEL numerical.

Ion implantation

  • Ions accelerated (10 keV – 5 MeV) and shot into the silicon
  • Range Rp = average implant depth (Gaussian distribution); ΔRp = straggle (std. deviation)
  • Crystal damage (amorphization) requires annealing (700–1000°C) to repair and activate dopants
  • RTA (Rapid Thermal Anneal): spikes to ~1050°C for <30 seconds — minimizes further diffusion
  • Advantage over thermal diffusion: precise dose control, lower temperature, maskable with photoresist

5.6 — Etching

TypeBehaviorExample
Wet etchIsotropic (etches all directions equally)HF for SiO₂; KOH for Si (anisotropic exception)
RIE (Reactive Ion Etch)Anisotropic — chemical + physical ion bombardmentStandard for gate/interconnect patterning
DRIE (Bosch process)Deep anisotropicTSV, MEMS structures

Selectivity = ratio of etch rates between target material, mask, and substrate (e.g. SiO₂:Si ~20:1 for CHF₃ plasma). Micro-loading: local etch rate depends on nearby pattern density.

5.7 — CMOS Fabrication Process Flow NPTEL Core — draw these from memory

Fig 5.3 — Simplified cross-section progression through the twin-well CMOS flow: well formation → STI isolation → gate stack → source/drain extension & spacers → heavy S/D implant + silicide → contacts/M1 → full BEOL metal stack → packaging. Being able to redraw this from memory is one of the most reliable ways to pass NPTEL fabrication questions.

Full step list (memorize the order)

  1. Starting material: p-type Si wafer
  2. n-well formation: phosphorus implant + anneal (for pMOS)
  3. STI formation: nitride deposition → trench etch → oxide fill → CMP
  4. Gate stack: dry-oxidize gate oxide (~1.5nm at 45nm node) → LPCVD poly-Si → pattern + etch
  5. Source/Drain extension (SDE): shallow, lightly doped, low-energy implant
  6. Spacer formation: SiO₂/Si₃N₄ CVD → anisotropic etch → sidewall spacers
  7. Source/Drain implant: heavy n+/p+ doping → RTA anneal
  8. Silicidation (SALICIDE): Ni deposition → anneal forms NiSi → strip unreacted Ni
  9. Contact Etch Stop Layer (CESL): strained nitride (tensile/compressive)
  10. Pre-Metal Dielectric (PMD): CVD SiO₂ → CMP planarize
  11. Contacts: etch holes → TiN barrier → W plug (CVD)
  12. BEOL metal (M1–M9+): dual-damascene — etch low-k dielectric → Cu electroplate → CMP; repeat per layer
  13. Passivation: SiO₂/Si₃N₄ over top metal, open bond pads
  14. Backend: dicing → wire bonding or flip-chip bumping → package

  • Draw the complete process cross-section from memory (all 10+ stages)
