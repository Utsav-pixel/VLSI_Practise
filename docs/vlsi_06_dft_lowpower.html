Phase 6 — DFT, Low Power Design & Custom Layout What separates senior VLSI engineers from junior ones. Duration: 8 weeks.

6.1 — Design for Testability (DFT) Industry Core

Once a chip is fabricated, you can't physically probe internal nodes of a billion-transistor die. DFT builds test access into the design from day one, targeting realistic manufacturing defect models.

Fault models

FaultDescription
Stuck-at (SA0/SA1)Node permanently stuck at 0 or 1 — the most common model used
BridgingTwo nodes accidentally shorted together
Transistor stuck-open/shortIndividual transistor fails to switch
Delay faultPath too slow — reflects real timing defects
Cell-aware faultDerived from actual transistor topology inside a cell

Scan design ⭐ most-used DFT technique

Fig 6.1 — Regular flip-flops are replaced with Scan Flip-Flops (extra SI input, SE select). With SE=1 the entire chain becomes one long shift register, giving external test equipment full controllability and observability of every internal state bit. Multiple parallel chains reduce test time on large designs.

BIST (Built-In Self Test)

  • LBIST (Logic BIST): on-chip PRPG (pattern generator) + MISR (signature analyzer)
  • MBIST (Memory BIST): tests SRAM/DRAM arrays automatically using March algorithms (March C-, March X, March Y) for stuck-at, coupling, and pattern-sensitivity faults

ATPG (Automatic Test Pattern Generation)

Algorithms (D-algorithm, PODEM, FAN) automatically generate test vectors targeting stuck-at faults. Fault coverage target: typically >98% in industry. Untestable faults usually come from redundant logic or tied-off nodes.

Boundary Scan (JTAG) — IEEE 1149.1

Tests interconnects between chips on a PCB via a 4-wire interface (TCK, TMS, TDI, TDO + optional TRST) and a TAP state machine. Also doubles as the debug port in most modern processors.

6.2 — Low Power VLSI Design Industry Critical

Ptotal = Pdynamic + Pstatic Pdynamic = α · CL · VDD² · f (switching power) Pstatic = I_leakage · VDD (leakage power — can EXCEED dynamic power below 28nm in idle blocks)

Dynamic power techniques

TechniqueHow it works
Voltage scalingPower ∝ VDD² — most effective lever, but delay rises as VDD drops toward Vth
DVFSDynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling together — used in every modern smartphone SoC
Clock gating ⭐ICG cell disables clock to idle registers — the single most-used technique
Operand isolationFreeze data inputs of a block when its output isn't needed, preventing spurious switching
Activity reductionGray-code counters (1 bit toggles/cycle vs. binary); bus encoding to cut transitions

Static (leakage) power techniques

Fig 6.2 — A header (pMOS) or footer (nMOS) switch disconnects an idle block's power rail entirely, cutting leakage to near-zero. Retention registers (SRPG) can keep critical state alive during power-down; wake-up must ramp current gradually to avoid Ldi/dt supply noise.
TechniqueNotes
Multi-Threshold CMOS (MTCMOS)High-Vth cells on non-critical paths (less leakage), low-Vth on critical paths (faster) — synthesis assigns Vth per cell automatically
Power gatingHeader/footer switches fully disconnect idle blocks (Fig 6.2)
Body biasingForward bias (FBB) lowers Vth for speed; reverse bias (RBB) raises Vth to cut leakage

Standards: UPF (Unified Power Format, IEEE 1801) defines power domains, voltage rails, isolation cells, and level shifters — this is what you'll actually write/read on the job (CPF is the older Cadence-specific equivalent).

6.3 — Custom Layout (Full Custom Design)

Standard cells get synthesized and placed automatically; full custom is reserved for analog circuits, memory bitcells, critical timing paths, and I/O pads — where minimum area, minimum delay, or precise matching matter enough to hand-draw.

Fig 6.3 — Canonical standard-cell layout: nMOS near the VSS rail, pMOS near the VDD rail, shared poly gate running vertically through both. Fixed height lets cells tile into rows during automatic placement; input/output pins sit on Metal1.

Verification steps (must know all three)

CheckVerifies
DRC (Design Rule Check)All geometry satisfies min width/spacing/enclosure/area rules
LVS (Layout vs. Schematic)Layout-extracted netlist matches the intended schematic netlist
ERC (Electrical Rule Check)Floating gates, unconnected inputs, other electrical issues
PEX / xRCExtracts real R, C parasitics from the layout for post-layout simulation
Lab (free, real process — Sky130 via Efabless):
  • Draw a CMOS inverter layout in Magic VLSI using the Sky130 PDK
  • Run DRC and fix all violations
  • Run LVS against the schematic, verify clean
  • Design and layout a 6T SRAM cell
  • Consider submitting a design to the Efabless Open MPW shuttle — free actual silicon fabrication
